How the maths works with your numbers

Blended rate

Each night's parcels are split across the bands and priced separately, then divided back out.

This is the number APC will look at. It falls as volume rises, which is what makes concentrating volume worth their while.

Payback

How long the site takes to earn back the machine. Annual revenue less running cost, divided into twelve, then divided into the capex.

Return on capital

Everything the site makes after tax across ten years, less what the machine cost, expressed against that cost.

NPV — net present value

Money arriving in year 8 is worth less than money today, because today's money could be earning elsewhere. NPV discounts every future year back to today, then subtracts what you put in.

Positive means the site beats the return you set as your hurdle. The discount rate should be what capital actually costs you — around 8% on asset finance, more if it's equity.

IRR — internal rate of return

The same sum run backwards: the rate at which NPV would come out at exactly zero. It's the annual return the site generates on its own.

Compare it straight to what money costs you. Borrow at 8% against a site returning more, and the gap is yours. A mobilisation fee lifts IRR sharply because early cash is worth far more to the calculation than the same amount in year six.

Which one to trust

IRR gives you the rate, NPV gives you the size. A small depot can show a spectacular IRR and still be worth little in absolute terms. A hub may return a lower percentage but far more money. NPV is what ends up in the bank.
