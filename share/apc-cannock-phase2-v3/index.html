02 · Service offering
— What we sell, to whom, and how we make money

Next day, nationwide, through ~88 member depots.

  • A high-quality, next-day parcel delivery business using a network of ~88 independent member depots and 3 APC-operated hubs to provide a nationwide service.
  • Members manage their own local customer accounts, parcel collection and parcel delivery in their territories.
  • Focused on the SME market, B2B and B2C. More exposed to B2B than the market, with ~40% from this segment. Differentiated by localised customer service through members and by handling specialised parcels such as security, fragile and liquid.
  • APC controls the trunking to and from members and the hub infrastructure. No other provider runs a network of independently owned and operated member businesses.
  • Freight from 25,000 to 30,000 shipping customers in any rolling 12 months, giving strength in depth across a wide range of markets and sectors.
Consignment split by product type

02 · Service offering
— Our USP

A model no one else runs.

APC’s member model is unique in the UK parcel market. Six advantages follow from it.

01

Service

Higher-touch, flexible and attentive service to smaller customers, such as a willingness to flex collection times. High-volume players do not focus on small and medium senders.

02

Sales relationships

Members are local businesses and build relationships with the SME community in their regions, where scaled companies focus on larger accounts.

03

One-stop shop

Members also offer same-day delivery, pallet transport and other services that the scaled parcel players cannot.

04

Cage delivery and collection network

A caged network with a bespoke cage design for safe, secure and efficient movement of freight in a closed loop.

05

Care and handling

More manual handling means fewer breakages on fragile consignments and the ability to carry irregular items such as flowers and liquids.

06

Delivery quality

Members rely on each other for service delivery, so each has a reason to keep delivery standards high.

03 · Market and competitor analysis
— Key trends and external forces

What is shaping demand.

Growing consumer demand for next-day deliveries
Prices continue to rise with fuel prices and driver wages
Government focus and right-to-work checks will shrink the driver market and raise driver costs
AI is speeding up customer-to-carrier integration and has removed the need for aggregators such as Metapak and Sorted
DHL, DX and DPD are seeing their service reputation and performance decline
Competitors are moving away from a personal customer service experience
Fewer carriers will take larger freight; InPost, for example, focuses on lockers for standard parcels
Customers want ease of use, carrier menu options, leave-safe, drop-off, sustainability and data security
Customer and consumer expectations of performance are higher than ever
Prices against diesel and driver pay

07 · IT roadmap
— Blue-sky thinking · owning APC’s core platform

Two routes to owning the platform.

Exploratory options, subject to business case, valuation and due diligence. Not included in the ~£6.0m plan.

Option 1

Acquire Hypaship

  • Proven platform already running APC’s bookings, depot operations, pricing, invoicing and APIs
  • Final mile brought onto the same platform, then extended into fulfilment
  • Brings the £1.2m annual spend in-house and keeps the margin in the group
  • Hypaship’s developers and platform knowledge come in-house
  • Licence revenue from existing customers, including Singapore Post and Jersey Post
Best fit if speed matters and the price is right
Option 2

Build our own platform

  • One platform for bookings and final mile, designed around APC’s network
  • A specialist partner builds it; APC owns the IP from day one
  • Modern, API-first architecture, so new channels plug in quickly
  • Replaces Hypaship and Openfleet costs over time
Best fit if APC wants a platform designed for its future model
Both routes
APC owns the IP and roadmap · one parcel record from booking to proof of delivery, feeding the AI agent · in-house apps (claims, lost property, service issues) consolidated, so fewer systems to run, maintain and secure

11 · Risk and mitigation
— Risks identified so far

What we are watching.

Sales, service and marketing
  • Competitive pricing from direct competitors: DPD, DHL, DX, FedEx
  • Significant service improvement and reliability from competitors
  • Economic retraction: APC is a good bellwether for the economy given its range of business and consumer customers
  • Member strength to operate profitably, grow with the business and keep service standards
  • Market price pressures (van drivers, derv, vehicle and premises costs) erode margin if not recovered
  • Peak service delivery: a good peak keeps customers for the long term
IT roadmap
  • Delivery capacity: a new hub build would compete with the IT roadmap for the same team. Hiring delays, unsettled programme governance and two key retirements could slow revenue projects unless extra capacity is planned in.
  • Cyber and resilience: attacks across logistics are growing, more sophisticated and faster as attackers use AI. APC’s 24/7 monitoring and controls are being strengthened through a prioritised year 1 programme.
  • Hypaship dependence: core growth projects rely on a supplier platform APC does not control, and its long-term future is still to be decided.
